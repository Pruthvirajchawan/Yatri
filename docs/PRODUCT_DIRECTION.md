# Product Direction

## Product Principle

GlobeTrotter should not merely answer:

> "What places should I visit?"

It should help answer:

> "Given my time, budget, preferences and group, what is the best realistic version of this trip?"

---

## Core Conceptual Models

### Experience Budget
Travelers have finite time and energy in addition to financial limits. Itinerary density must be calibrated against human fatigue, transit friction, and downtime needed to appreciate destinations.

### Trip Debt
Repeated compromises and over-packed days can make an itinerary technically possible on paper, but practically exhausting in execution. Unaddressed trip debt leads to burnout and ruined experiences.

### Regret Map
A trip should actively consider what a traveler is most likely to regret missing versus what can be safely skipped or substituted based on personal taste and unrepeatable opportunities.

### Group Equity
A group trip should not silently optimize for only the loudest or most assertive traveler's preferences. It must balance compromises transparently across all participants.

### Trade-Off Engine
Every major itinerary decision carries explicit trade-offs. The system exposes what the traveler gains and what they sacrifice when choosing faster transit, cheaper lodging, or additional stops.

### Travel DNA
The system should eventually learn from actual traveler behavior, pacing, and post-trip choices rather than relying exclusively on static onboarding surveys.

### Domino Effect Simulator
Changing one part of a trip inevitably ripples through transportation connections, booked activities, budget margins, and downstream scheduling. The simulator forecasts and mitigates these cascading consequences.
